## How the core loop actually works

Strip away the marketing language and the loop is a decision simulator with progression attached.

1. **A car is offered.** You do not browse a lot or negotiate. The game presents a vehicle.
2. **You accept or decline.** Accepting adds the car to your collection and, per the description, contributes to your money and progress. Declining passes on it.
3. **Your collection and money grow.** Those two resources appear to be the levers that unlock more cars and additional garage floors.
4. **Rarity becomes the goal.** The advertised end state is the rarest collection, which is why the "diamond" cars and prestige levels were highlighted in the latest update.

The interesting design tension is that accept-or-decline is a *commitment* mechanic. If garage space is limited, or if money matters for unlocking floors, then a car that looks exciting early may not be the right pick. The description implies this trade-off exists but never spells out the numbers, so the only reliable way to learn the thresholds is to play and watch what changes after each decision.

## What third-party guides add — and how much to trust them

Several community-written guides cover this experience. They are useful for orientation, particularly if you are new and want a sense of what the early game feels like, but they are **secondary observations, not developer documentation**. That means:

- Any specific vehicle name, price, or rarity tier you see in a fan guide should be treated as that author's observation from their own play session, not as confirmed game data.
- Community guides are frequently written against an older build. The September 27, 2026 update added diamond cars and new prestiges, which means anything published before that date may be describing a version of the game that no longer exists in the same form.
- Aggregator pages, dictionary entries, and unrelated products that happen to share words like "car collection" are noise. They tell you nothing about this experience.

The safest habit is to read a community guide for the *shape* of the game — the loop, the general pacing — and then verify every concrete number yourself in-game.
