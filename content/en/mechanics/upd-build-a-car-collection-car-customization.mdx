## The Core Loop: Accept or Decline

According to the game's official description, the central interaction is simple and deliberate. Cars are presented to you one at a time, and you either accept or decline each one. That single decision is the engine of the whole experience.

Accepting a car adds it to your personal collection. The description frames the broader goal as earning in-game money, expanding that collection, unlocking more cars, unlocking more floors of the garage, and ultimately hunting down rare and expensive supercars. The stated ambition is to "build the rarest car collection of them all."

This is worth reading carefully, because it distinguishes the experience from a racing game or a driving sandbox. Nothing in the official description promises racing, handling physics, tuning, or competitive driving. The advertised activity is choosing, collecting, and progressing. If you arrive expecting a driving simulator, the accept-or-decline framing is your clearest warning that the priorities lie elsewhere.

The two-player-facing systems the developer does name are money and collection size. Both are described as things that grow, and both are tied to the act of accepting cars. That suggests a risk-and-reward layer: declining a car presumably preserves the option to see something better, while accepting locks in progress. The official page does not explain the exact trade-off, and it is not documented anywhere authoritative, so treat any confident claim about optimal declining strategy as unverified.
