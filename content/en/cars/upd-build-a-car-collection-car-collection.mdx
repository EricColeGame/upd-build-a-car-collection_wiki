## The core loop: accept or decline

The most important mechanic the developer states outright is the accept/decline decision. Cars are presented to you, and you decide whether to take each one. That single choice is where the game's strategy lives, and it explains why the experience plays more like a decision simulator than a driving game.

What is *not* documented is the exact consequence of declining. The official description does not say whether a declined car is gone forever, returns later, or is replaced immediately by the next offer. Different players report different impressions, which is a good sign that the detail is either random, update-dependent, or simply not published. The safe assumption is that accepting adds a car to your collection and declining passes on it — but verify the rest in your own session rather than trusting a guide.

Money is the other half of the loop. The description confirms you earn money and use it to expand your collection, but it does not state how money is earned. It could be tied to accepting cars, to time played, to collection size, or to a mix. This is genuinely undocumented, so watch your currency counter after each action in-game to learn the pattern yourself.

## Diamond cars, prestiges, and the rarity chase

The phrase "diamond cars" appears in the developer's own update announcement, so it is legitimate to talk about them as a category. What "diamond" means mechanically is not explained on the official page. It may indicate a tier above the usual rarity ladder, a special visual variant, or something tied to prestige progress. Community guides speculate about this constantly, but speculation is not documentation.

The same caution applies to prestiges. The developer advertises "new prestiges," which implies a reset-and-reward system of some kind, since that is what prestige usually means in collection simulators. The official description does not state what resets, what carries over, or what a prestige grants. If you are chasing prestige levels, the reliable method is to check the in-game UI for a prestige tab or prompt and read exactly what it promises before committing.

Supercars deserve a similar note. "Find rare and expensive supercars" is a stated goal of the game, which effectively confirms that rarity tiers exist and that the rarest cars are the long-term target. It does not confirm which cars are rarest or how likely they are to appear. There is no published drop-rate table, and no responsible guide should invent one.

## How to verify anything yourself

Because so much is undocumented, self-verification is the practical skill here:

1. Read the game description on the Roblox page before playing, and re-read it after major updates.
2. Watch the offer screen: note what happens to a declined car across several offers.
3. Track your money before and after specific actions to infer the earning rule.
4. Check the garage for locked floors and see whether the game states an unlock requirement.
5. Look for any in-game prestige or diamond indicator and read its tooltip rather than assuming.

None of this guarantees a fast collection or a high prestige level. The game involves chance, since cars are offered rather than chosen freely, and the developer controls the pool. What you can control is your accept/decline discipline and your understanding of the loop.
