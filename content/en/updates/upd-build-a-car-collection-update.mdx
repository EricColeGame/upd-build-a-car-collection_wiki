If you have seen the phrase "[UPD] Build a Car Collection!" and wondered what it actually refers to, the short answer is that it is a Roblox experience — not a real-world car-buying guide. The game is published by the group Fountain Entertainment and lives at a single official place page: [Build a Car Collection on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/139148782992676/Build-a-Car-Collection). The "UPD" tag is a common Roblox convention signaling that the developer recently pushed an update.

## What the game officially advertises

The developer's own description is the authority here, and it is fairly compact. It advertises a new update featuring new cars, "diamond" cars, and new prestige levels. The core loop, as described, is simple: cars are offered to you, and you choose to accept or decline each one. Accepting and progressing earns money and expands your collection. Growth unlocks more cars and more floors of your garage. The stated goal is to find rare and expensive supercars and build the rarest collection of them all.

That is the complete official feature list. Notably, the description does not name individual vehicles, list prices, publish drop rates, explain prestige requirements, or mention a code redemption system. If you see a claim that contradicts any of that, the claim did not come from the developer.

## The accept-or-decline loop, explained

The defining mechanic is the offer. Rather than driving around a world hunting for vehicles, you are presented with a car and asked to make a yes-or-no decision. This is the structure the developer describes, and it shapes how you should think about the game.

Because the official page does not explain the consequences of declining, the safest assumption is the one the description supports: your choice affects what enters your collection and how your money grows. Whether declining carries a penalty, costs a reroll, or simply moves you to the next offer is not stated. The reliable way to find out is to play a few offers and watch your money and collection counters — the game shows you the result immediately, so you never need to rely on a guide for that.

The same applies to the "expand your collection" promise. The description says collections grow and that growth unlocks more cars and more floors. It does not say how many cars you need, or which floor unlocks when. Those are exactly the kinds of numbers third-party sites love to invent, and exactly the kinds the developer has not published.

## Money, floors, and prestige

Two progression tracks are advertised: money and collection size. Money is earned through the loop, and collection size appears to be the gate that opens new floors and new car offerings. The description frames unlocking as a reward for expanding, which strongly suggests collection size is the key metric rather than playtime.

Prestige is mentioned only as "new prestiges" arriving in the update. That tells you prestige exists and was recently expanded. It does not tell you what prestige costs, what it resets, or what it grants. If you are the type of player who likes to plan a prestige run in advance, this is the part of the game where you will have to experiment rather than follow a spreadsheet.

Diamond cars are likewise advertised but unexplained. The name suggests a tier above the standard offerings, but the developer has not described how diamond cars appear, whether they are rarer offers, or whether they require a specific garage state. Treat any specific claim about diamond car odds as unverified.
